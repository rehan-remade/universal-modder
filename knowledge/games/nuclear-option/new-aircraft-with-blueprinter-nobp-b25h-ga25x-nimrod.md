# Three new flyable aircraft in Nuclear Option with Blueprinter .nobp

One Blueprinter addon (`heritage_pairs`) adds three new aircraft next to the stock ones: a B-25H Mitchell, the
GA-25X (a fictional 2080s rebuild of the same airframe), and a Hawker Nimrod Mk I carrier biplane. Each aircraft
is generated by a Unity editor script in the Blueprinter project, run in batch mode, which writes one `.nobp`.
The user played all three in single player and accepted them. They tested flight, AI take-off and landing,
weapons, damage and break-up, cockpits, carrier hook landings and the HUD. An earlier route that rebuilt a stock
aircraft at runtime with a BepInEx plugin was rejected; Gotcha 1 explains why.

## Setup
- **Game:** Nuclear Option 0.34.3 (Steam, Windows 11), Unity 2022.3.62f2 Mono, no anti-cheat.
- **Loaders:** BepInEx 5.4.23.5 and Blueprinter 2.1.1. The user already had several community aircraft mods
  installed, and our addon coexists with them.
- **Blueprinter editor project** (NOBlueprinter-Editor) opened with **Unity 2022.3.62f2 exactly**:
  - Batch mode needs Unity Hub running, because its licence client holds the signed-in licence. Without it
    batch mode fails with "No valid Unity Editor license found".
  - Batch command:
    `Unity.exe -batchmode -nographics -quit -projectPath <project> -executeMethod <Class.Method> -logFile <log>`.
- **AssetRipper export** of the game, made by the user, kept outside every repo. It is the reference for stock
  prefab values (YAML) and decompiled script bodies (to read how the game works). Nothing from it ships.
- **Other tools:** Blender 5.2.2 for procedural models and bakes, and fal for paint textures, decals, cockpit
  art and a few image-to-3D props. A small BepInEx plugin of ours handles the things a prefab can't do; see
  "How the game works" below.
- **Install layout:** `<game>\BepInEx\plugins\com.nikkorap.blueprinter\addons\<addon id>\` holds exactly one
  `<Name>_<version>.nobp` plus a `meta.json`.
  - The `meta.json` schema is the same as other addons: id, artifact fileName, version, gameVersion, the
    `extends` entry for Blueprinter, and `hash` = `sha256:<nobp sha256>`.
  - Copy the `meta.json` that the build writes. A stale version or hash in it confuses the loader.

## Route and why
- **First try: runtime morph** (BepInEx plugin, at `Encyclopedia.AfterLoad`):
  - It cloned the stock A-19 (`CAS1`), moved every physics part onto our model and registered a new key.
  - It flew, but the user rejected it as "a re-skinned A-19". It kept the A-19's liveries, pylons, cockpit,
    audio and interior, had hollow insides when parts broke off, and both props drove the same hub.
- **Final route: Blueprinter `.nobp`.** This is the community route.
  - Our generator scripts build each aircraft prefab from scratch in the Blueprinter project. Stock systems
    are taken by cloning individual *components* from stock prefabs (engine, gear, guns, damage, avionics).
  - The scripts also write the `AircraftDefinition`, the `AircraftParameters` (airfoils, liveries, loadouts,
    HUD) and the hangar operations, then call Blueprinter's `ModBuilder.Build`.
  - Everything is regenerated from scripts on every build, so a full rebuild of three aircraft takes about
    5 minutes.
- **Plugin for the rest:** a separate small BepInEx plugin (Harmony) covers runtime behaviour a prefab can't
  express: live cockpit gauges, a fixed-gear HUD fix, hook visuals, crash-gear rules and diagnostics. It only
  touches our own aircraft (checked by `definition.jsonKey`).

## How the game works (what we had to learn)
Symbols are from 0.34.3; the behaviour is described in my own words.

**Aircraft structure**
- An aircraft is a tree of `AeroPart`s. Each has a mass, `wingArea`, `dragArea`, an airfoil index into
  `AircraftParameters.airfoils`, a `liftNormal` transform, `centerOfLift` and `PartJoint`s with
  `breakForce`/`breakTorque`.
- In complex physics every part is its own rigidbody joined by joints. Lift acts at each surface's own lift
  point, so a biplane's stagger and gap are real in the physics.

**Control surfaces**
- `ControlSurface` rotates the surface's `visibleMesh` transform. When that transform is the hinged part's
  `liftNormal`, the surface part's angle of attack becomes the wing's AoA plus the *full* deflection, looked
  up on the part's airfoil curve. See Gotcha 9.

**Fly-by-wire**
- `ControlsFilter.FlyByWire` `alphaLimiter` measures the *root (fuselage)* AoA and only scales a commanded
  pitch rate. Wing incidence adds on top of it.

**Damage**
- Damage is per part. `UnitPart.TakeDamage` divides by armour tolerances.
- An `AeroPart`'s joints weaken as hit points fall toward `structuralThreshold`; at zero the part comes off.
- `integrityThreshold` spawns fragments.
- Collision damage comes from `AeroPart.OnCollisionEnter`: the impulse is turned into g on that part's own
  mass, minus `impactDamage.threshold`, times `multiplier`. **Impact damage is skipped entirely when
  `threshold` is 0.** See Gotcha 11.

**Landing gear** (`LandingGear`)
- Contact is a Linecast down from a `castPoint`. It applies spring and damper forces (`springRate`,
  `dampingRate`, `suspensionTravel`).
- It calls `BreakWheel` when compression exceeds `maxCompression`, when the hinge angle passes about 10°, on
  a soft-ground force check, or when its part detaches. Stock limits sit at 72–83% of travel.
- "Retracting" disables ground contact. A fixed undercarriage still has gear state, so the G key, air spawns
  and the AI can "retract" it.

**HUD landing mode**
- While `aircraft.gearDeployed` is true, the HUD runs in landing mode:
  - `HUDUnitMarker.UpdateHidden` hides every non-aircraft marker (ground, ships, missiles);
  - `HUDBoresightState` hides the gun pipper;
  - the HUD weapon states hide the target designator, and the bombing state drops the impact (CCIP) line.
- Fixed-gear aircraft therefore need help; see Gotcha 12.

**Bombs**
- `Missile.DetectCollisions` Linecasts ahead from the bomb's transform. A hit on the bomb's *own* rigidbody
  makes it return without testing anything else.
- `OpticalSeekerBomb` also detonates a slow, low, armed bomb on its own timer.
- Over water a sea-level check runs first.

**Hangars and carriers**
- Hangars and carriers offer an aircraft through hangar operations in the definition. Each carrier deck spot
  has its own size limit and spawn behaviour.
- Only the Hyperion-class carrier has arrester wires.
- `TailHook` and `ArrestorGear` handle the catch. The hook's line of pull versus the CG decides whether the
  aircraft noses over during the arrest.

**Liveries**
- Liveries must be *mod-local* `LiveryData` assets, referenced by an addressable GUID from `AircraftParameters`.
- A stock `_Livery` texture is the complete painted base colour.

**Blueprinter placeholders**
- Stock assets are referenced through Blueprinter's `*_PLACEHOLDER` assets (prefabs, `WeaponInfo`,
  `AircraftParameters`, effects). They resolve to the real stock asset at load.
- A placeholder `NetworkIdentity` is a missing script: strip it and add the real component.

## Build steps
1. Back up saves (`um backup`) and the BepInEx config before the first modded launch.
2. Make a smoke-test mod first: copy a stock aircraft under a new key, give it its own `LiveryData` and a
   hangar operation, build with `ModBuilder.Build`, and install. A "(addon) Loaded definition <KEY>" line in
   `LogOutput.log` plus the aircraft in a hangar proves the pipeline.
3. Generate the model in Blender from a script. Export one FBX plus a JSON contract: part names, pivots, hinge
   axes, gear axles, muzzles, hardpoints and cockpit movers.
4. In the Blueprinter project, write an editor script per aircraft. It should:
   - import the FBX;
   - build `AeroPart`s, joints, control surfaces, gear, engine/prop, guns, mounts and the cockpit by cloning
     stock components (`Object.Instantiate`, or copying serialized fields);
   - write the definition, parameters, airfoils and liveries;
   - run its own checks (an `R(name, ok, detail)` list printed as `CHECK ALL PASS` or `FAIL`).
5. A family script builds all aircraft, then calls `ModBuilder.Build(modId, displayName, version, outFolder)`
   and writes `meta.json` with the sha256.
6. Install: replace the `.nobp` and `meta.json` in the addon folder, keeping one `.nobp` per folder. Restart
   the game.

## Verification
- **Offline:**
  - Blender model checks: connectivity of struts and wires, see-through with backface culling from 42
    directions and from inside every break, z-fighting, control-surface deflection clearance, face winding on
    revolved parts, every cockpit needle visible from the eye.
  - Unity build checks per aircraft: 37–39 for the Nimrod.
  - Python stubs that re-implement the game's equations: flight model (speed, climb, stall, take-off), roll
    authority vs AoA, hook arrest and nose-over, gear drop test, bomb-fuse Linecast. A sync test makes sure
    the C# constants equal the stub constants.
- **In game:**
  - A throwaway test-helper plugin driven by a command file: external camera, inputs, AI pilot, state logs.
    Remove it after every session; left installed, it was the cause of a "lag" report.
  - The user's own test sessions with screenshots. Diagnostic log lines from our plugin, for example one line
    per bomb detonation with its height above the ground, proved the bomb fix.
- **Not verified:** multiplayer sync of the plugin-side behaviours. Single player only.

## Gotchas
1. **Runtime-morphed stock aircraft looked like the donor.**
   - **Cause:** liveries, pylons, cockpit, audio and the part interiors all come from the donor prefab.
   - **Fix:** build the prefab yourself in the Blueprinter project and clone only the *components* you need.
2. **Batch-mode Unity: "No valid Unity Editor license found".**
   - **Cause:** Unity Hub wasn't running.
   - **Fix:** start Hub (it holds the licence), then run batch mode.
   - Also check where the log went: a bash wrapper that wrote `...\unitylogs\$2.log` inside double quotes
     created a file literally named `$2.log`.
3. **Liveries didn't show, or the A-19's showed instead.**
   - **Cause:** liveries must be mod-local `LiveryData` assets.
   - **Fix:** create one `LiveryData` per livery in the mod folder and reference it by GUID.
4. **A see-through cockpit shell.**
   - **Cause:** the stock interior-glass shader (canopy_int) on our canopy turned the cockpit view into an
     opaque dark shell.
   - **Fix:** don't copy the glass onto the cockpit layer.
5. **Parts looked hollow on break-up, and struts and fins floated free.**
   - **Cause:** one-sided skins and nothing behind the split.
   - **Fix:**
     - two-sided or backed skins;
     - frames, ribs and stringer stubs behind every split, with complementary break edges;
     - a connectivity check (every strut, wire and fin end within 1 cm of its surface);
     - a see-through check with culling on.
6. **A prop hub was see-through from outside but visible from inside.**
   - **Cause:** a lathe helper picked face winding from the first ring pair with a non-zero score. The
     spinner's first band is a flat disc whose score is rounding noise, so 448 of 512 faces came out inverted.
   - **Fix:** orient every band from the profile itself (the winding of the profile polygon closed with the
     axis), and add an "all faces outward" check.
7. **Bombs always burst 3–5 m above land, never over water.**
   - **Cause:** the bomb's pivot was its suspension lug, a few millimetres outside its own capsule. The impact
     Linecast started outside and hit the bomb itself, so the ground was never tested. The bomb bounced and
     the slow-and-low timer fired it in the air.
   - **Fix:** pivot at the capsule centre. Stock bombs have centred pivots.
   - Also use the stock yield rule (blastYield ≈ 0.8 × mass for unguided bombs) and the matching stock effect
     family.
8. **Synchronised guns shot our own prop.**
   - **Cause:** bullet Linecasts start at the muzzle and hit the prop-strike trigger.
   - **Fix:** put synchroniser bullet origins about 0.2 m ahead of the prop plane, with the flash particles
     at the real muzzle.
9. **A biplane stalled in gentle turns and couldn't roll in a turn.**
   - **Cause 1:** the aileron parts used the wing airfoil. The game adds the full 18° deflection to the
     aileron part's AoA, so from about 10° wing AoA the down aileron was past CLmax and made *less* lift than
     neutral (roll reversal).
   - **Fix 1:** give the ailerons their own airfoil that behaves like a plain flap: CL keeps rising to about
     1.85 at 30°, with flap-like drag.
   - **Cause 2:** `alphaLimiter` (root AoA) 16.5° plus 1.5° wing incidence put the wings at 18°, past CLmax.
   - **Fix 2:** limiter + incidence = the wing's CLmax angle, and a gentle post-stall curve (6% lift loss
     5° past CLmax instead of 18%).
10. **Fixed gear snapped on every landing.**
    - **Cause:** the springs were too soft and the leg broke at 52% of travel. Stock aircraft are much stiffer
      and break at 72–83% of travel.
    - **Fix:** stock-like stiffness (200 kN/m and 20 kN·s/m on a 1.8 t aircraft) and a break limit at 87% of
      travel.
    - Our plugin also logs every `BreakWheel` on our aircraft with the compression and speeds, and blocks it
      outside a crash. The log line is how you find the real cause in a user's session.
11. **A 500 km/h dive into the ground only knocked the nose off.**
    - **Cause:** most stock part templates have `impactDamage.threshold` 0 (impact damage off). Only the
      fuselage-type parts have it on.
    - **Fix:** give every part its *own* `ImpactDamage` instance (a cloned field shares the template's
      object): fabric 20 g × 1.5 and metal 30 g × 1.
    - Gear legs that hang on the root part never detach, so a plugin rule breaks them on a large fuselage
      impact.
12. **No ground, ship or missile markers, no gun sight, no designator in a fixed-gear aircraft.**
    - **Cause:** HUD landing mode keys on `gearDeployed`, and a fixed-gear aircraft is always gear-down.
    - **Fix:** a Harmony prefix sets `UpdateHidden(gearExtended)` to false for our aircraft. The HUD
      weapon-state methods run with the `gearDeployed` field cleared; restore it in a *finalizer* so an
      exception can't leave it changed.
    - Wheels and the network value are untouched. Weapon `GearSafety` must be off on the mounts too.
13. **The G key, air spawns or the AI "retracted" a fixed undercarriage and killed ground contact.**
    - **Fix:** the plugin calls `SetGear(true)` again on the aircraft you simulate locally, and the gear's fold
      angle is 0.5° (0 divides by zero in the fold animation) so nothing visibly moves.
14. **A taildragger nosed over on carrier arrests.**
    - **Cause:** the hook's line of pull passed below the CG at the 3-point attitude.
    - **Fix:** move the hook hinge so the line of pull stays within −0.25 m of the CG at 0–7.5° pitch and
      −0.5 m at the 3-point attitude. Model it offline first.
15. **Red and green lines glowed along the aileron hinges.**
    - **Cause:** the stock nav-light Point lights (URP per-pixel) lit the paint faces in the hinge slot.
    - **Fix:** place the lights well outboard with a short range, or (on a 1930s aircraft) use no dynamic
      lights at all.
16. **"Lag" reports.**
    - **Cause:** our test-helper DLL had been left in `plugins`.
    - **Fix:** remove it after every test, and check before handing the game back.
17. **The Store build of Unity Hub has no reachable CLI.**
    - **Fix:** install the exact editor version yourself and call `Unity.exe` directly.
18. **The game must be closed to swap the plugin.**
    - **Cause:** a running game locks the plugin DLL. A `.nobp` can be replaced on disk, but it only loads at
      start.
    - **Fix:** queue the DLL copy until the game process exits, matching it by PID.

## Assets
- **Models:** Blender Python generators. Every part is built procedurally from a dimension spec and checked
  by script.
- **Reference models:** a War Thunder rip was used as a *visual reference only*: proportions, comparison
  renders and silhouette IoU. No geometry or texture from it ships.
- **fal art:**
  - paint and detail textures (`google/nano-banana-2.1`), projected in 3D by baking world position per texel
    so seams don't show;
  - cockpit placards and dials;
  - HUD weapon icons drawn from our own models in the stock icon format (512 × 256, white line art on black).
- **Bake gotcha:** a Cycles multi-object bake into one image applies the margin per object, so one object's
  margin overwrites another's islands. Bake with margin 0 and dilate afterwards.
- **Cockpits:** kitbash the stock seat, stick, throttle and displays where the aircraft allows. Custom-model
  only what's period-specific.
